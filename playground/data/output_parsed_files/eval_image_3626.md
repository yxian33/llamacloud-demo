• In emerging markets, governments brace for post-election fiscal challenges.

• **South Africa:** Although Finance Minister Enoch Godongwana's recent budget release limited the possibility of significant pre-election fiscal populism, some politically difficult decisions have been delayed until after the election, such as establishing permanent funding for the social programme (R350 SRD grant).

• **Mexico:** This year's budget appears to reflect a strategy of consolidating already-high presidential approval ratings, as it prioritises social spending. After the election, we think a fiscal reform will be necessary to avoid non-containable expenditures straining the sustainability of public finances.

• **India:** The government plans to reduce the fiscal deficit to 4.5% of GDP by FY26 and has reiterated that announced measures are "more than adequate to get a credit ratings upgrade". However, India's public debt/GDP ratio currently sits nearly 30pp above the BBB median of 56%, and as it is likely to stay above 80% even in FY26, we think the government will have to focus on rebuilding its fiscal buffer.
