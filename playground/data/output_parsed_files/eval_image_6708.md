• We expect a contractionary fiscal impulse across the four largest eurozone economies in 2024, though the headline metric exaggerated by the automatic expiration of energy-support measures and exclusion of EU grants.

• Further muddling the policy outlook is the reimplementation of reformed EU fiscal rules. While we expect this to have minimal implications for this year, the rules will require an extended period of tighter fiscal policy.

• The extent of consolidation required over the next two to three years remains unclear due to mitigations for defence spending, EU funds and higher interest costs each country is to determine bilaterally with the European Commission.

• Ahead of the European Parliament elections (see page 33), we have seen a distinct shift in policy priorities from the green transition to defence. On the latter proposals include increasing national defence spending – an upside risk for deficits – and using the European Investment Bank. Some policymakers have proposed joint borrowing for defence, but we consider this more politically challenging.
