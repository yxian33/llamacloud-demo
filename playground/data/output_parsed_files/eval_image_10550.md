* The stagflationary risk of increased US protectionism, discussed in our Global Outlook 2024, seems to us more likely in the event of a win for Trump.

* Were the US to impose a new wave of tariffs, we think the EU would respond swiftly, but within international rules and balancing economics and politics.

* On the one hand, the EU has bolstered its economic defence mechanisms in recent years, and support for parties focused on strategic autonomy seems to be increasing among voters. On the other hand, the US now accounts for more of EU trade, so we think such a stance would imply a higher cost than before.

* Mexico's economic cycle is highly integrated with the US industrial cycle, and we believe Mexico will continue to play a prominent role in nearshoring trends. However, a second Trump presidency might result in a tougher stance on trade and stricter rules of origin when the USMCA free-trade agreement is due for revision in 2026.

* On the security and migration front, a second Trump term could put strain on the relationship, especially given increased fentanyl flows into the US. Stronger anti-migrant rhetoric could have an impact on remittance inflows to Mexico, which accounted for 3.8% of its GDP in 2023.
