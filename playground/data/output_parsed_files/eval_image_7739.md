# EM elections: Status quo for most, tail risks for South Africa

## Mexico: President and Congress
2 June 2024

Opinion polls indicate a victory for the incumbent Morena-led alliance's presidential candidate, Claudia Sheinbaum. The alliance looks likely to retain enough of a majority in Congress to approve the budget and secondary laws.

While we see little room for Sheinbaum to deviate significantly from the status quo, we believe the economic context will demand more active policies in terms of energy transition and private investment as well as, potentially, a new fiscal reform.

## South Africa: Parliament
29 May 2024

Despite some indications to the contrary from volatile opinion polls, our base case remains that the African National Congress will slip below a 50% vote share but form a comfortable governing alliance.

We expect a second term for President Cyril Ramaphosa at the head of a more centrist government, better able to double down on deregulation and crowd in private investment, bolstering growth and ZAR asset performance. That said, lack of a majority win for the incumbent government would present its fair share of tail risks, we think.

## India: Parliament
April–May 2024

According to opinion polls, Prime Minister Narendra Modi's National Democratic Alliance looks set to retain its majority in the parliament. In our view, recent victories in state elections and restrained spending announcements in the interim budget underline the incumbent's confidence.

Overall, we expect policy continuity in India. The government looks set to keep to its fiscal consolidation path, with a target fiscal deficit of 4.5% of GDP by FY2025-26, and to continue its efforts to encourage infrastructure development.

## South Korea: Parliament
10 April 2024

Latest opinion polls suggest a close race between the two main parties and scope for a narrower seat distribution than at present. The Democratic Party of Korea remains the larger party, but President Yoon Suk Yeol's People Power Party gained momentum in February alongside the president's own approval rating.

We do not expect the election result to have a material impact on policy, with both parties likely to continue to emphasise fiscal soundness. Given weak fiscal revenue conditions, we see limited room to increase fiscal spending.
