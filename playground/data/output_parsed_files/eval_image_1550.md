## What's >1k TWh net load growth or a 25% increase by 2033 imply for capacity?

We assume 60 / 25 / 15 split for gas / PV / onshore wind TWh contribution. We calculate a ~40% grid limit from renewables (higher in specific regions, reflects aggregate, and is net of batteries). PV vs. wind split roughly reflects interconnection queue mix.

**Natural Gas:** +631 TWh from 10 yr. of load growth (in addition to growth from coal retirements). Equal to +35% increase in total gas-power TWh compared to 2023. Equals +120 GW needed at 60% capacity factor or avg. +12 GW / yr. (if all came from new installs instead of higher utilization of existing fleet). ~9 GW / yr has been the run rate added, without load growth as a key driver.

**Solar PV:** +263 TWh from 10 yr. of load growth (in addition to growth from decarbonization of grid). Equals +150 GW needed at 20% capacity factor or avg. +15 GW / yr. This is on top of 25-40 GW / year forward estimates related to decarbonization (23 GW new installs in 2023).

**Onshore Wind:** +158 TWh from 10 yr. of load growth (in addition to growth from decarbonization of grid). Equals +50 GW needed at 35% capacity factor or avg. +5 GW / yr. This is on top of 10-20 GW / year forward estimates related to decarbonization (7 GW new installs in 2023).
