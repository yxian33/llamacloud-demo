• **China to continue exporting deflationary pressure**: Overcapacity and still weak domestic demand amid depressed consumer confidence will keep China's PPI deflation at 1.5% y/y in 2024, we expect, after 3% y/y in 2023. This means global goods prices should continue to fall at least up to the summer, with possible downside risks (see page 28).

• **Still direct negative contribution from energy prices**: Falls in energy prices should be especially evident in Europe, where lower gas prices work with a longer lag. We expect negative contributions from energy in European PPIs for most of the year: about -0.3pp for the eurozone and -1pp for the UK, compared with -0.1pp and +0.7pp last year, respectively.

• **Ongoing spillover to rest of price chain**: As energy is a significant cost component for many sectors, lower energy prices imply softer prices in food and highly energy-intensive sectors.
