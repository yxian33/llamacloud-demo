

## Pensions and unemployment benefits: 
Two recent reforms will contribute to lower spending. According to government estimates, the pension reform will trim the deficit by around 0.5% of GDP and the unemployment benefit reform by 0.2% of GDP by 2028. This represents about one-third of the fiscal effort we estimate is required to stabilise the public debt ratio.

## Many spending priorities: 
However, spending is planned to increase in many categories in the coming years, such as in healthcare, education, security, justice, and defence. It will also be difficult to trim spending in family and employment policies.

## Tough choices ahead: 
There seems to be some leeway to cut spending in economic affairs (e.g. subsidies to companies) and housing, two areas where France spends more than its eurozone peers. But here too, some spending may be hard to cut. R&D tax credits explain a large part of the difference in the economic affairs category, but the government will likely be reluctant to reduce those when the priority is to promote innovation. It may also be hard to cut subsidies to the housing sector, which is already hit by the interest rate cycle.
