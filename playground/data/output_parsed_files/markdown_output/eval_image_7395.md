

**Rate shock:** France has mainly fixed-rate mortgages, but its housing sector is highly sensitive to changes in interest rates. The c.3pp rise in the average mortgage rate since mid-2022 has been the largest rate shock since the early 1980s and has triggered a sharp decline in net credit flows, to almost zero in H2 2023.

**Housing starts in sharp fall:** The plunge in housing demand has depressed housing starts to 25-year lows. The latest survey from homebuilders suggests some stabilisation. Although demand for house renovation has been more resilient, we expect residential investment to decline for a third year in a row in 2024. The peak-to-trough decline could exceed 10%, according to our estimates.

**Mortgage rates to decline soon:** French mortgage rates mainly adjust to the smoothed 20-year OAT yield. Given our forecast for long-term yields, we think mortgage rates will decline from March onwards. This could start unfreezing credit flows and unlocking some pent-up demand this year. Nevertheless, we forecast mortgage rates to remain above 3% by end-2025, way above the c.1% recorded in the aftermath of the pandemic.
