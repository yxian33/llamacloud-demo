* The US election could catalyze changes around our base case of fiscal drag in 2024 and a minimal impulse in 2025. The menu of post-election policy options will depend on the balance of power in Congress: both parties agree on extending tax cuts for lower earners, but Republicans are likely to push for a full extension when cuts expire in 2025.

* Constructive compromise on spending and revenue-raising initiatives is possible when different parties hold the Congress and the presidency. However, as in 2011, a mix of a Republican Congress and Democratic president could mean spending cuts in exchange for lifting the debt limit — dragging on growth in the process.

* The reduction in the overall US corporate tax rate in 2017 brought its statutory federal rate more in line with its peers at 21% (see bottom left-hand chart). Biden has proposed lifting the rate to 28%, while advisers to Trump have floated a further reduction to 15%.
