* Central banks are weighing up the risks of cutting rates prematurely against the risks of cutting too late.

* While a 1970s-style re-acceleration of inflationary pressures looks unlikely, history nonetheless warns of premature celebrations.

* In our view, re-acceleration risk remains most prominent in the US and, as a result, the risk of premature Fed easing outweighs that of its being behind the curve on the way down.

* These concerns look more valid in the US than they do in the eurozone, with the latter facing a vastly different macroeconomic backdrop. The ECB does not need to wait for the Fed before starting to ease policy.

* The argument for imminent ECB rate cuts is more compelling than for the Fed, in our view, with eurozone inflation likely to undershoot the 2% target in H2 and our BVAR model suggesting near-term downside risks to core inflation.

* Even so, given improving activity data and resilient wage growth, we think the ECB will exercise patience before beginning its cutting cycle in June.
