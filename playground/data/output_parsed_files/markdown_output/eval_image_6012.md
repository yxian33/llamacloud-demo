# US trade policy: China would take the brunt of a Trump presidency

## Risk of escalating tensions
China's relationship with the US deteriorated dramatically during the Trump presidency. In our view, a second term for Trump would risk straining the relationship again, both economically and geopolitically. On the campaign trail, he has floated imposing a tariff of 60% or more on all Chinese imports and revoking its status as a most favored nation in trade terms.

## Damage of higher tariffs
The increase in average tariffs from 3% to 12% during Trump's term in office materially brought down China's market share of US imports, from 21.6% in 2017 to 16.1% in 2023, though China has probably recouped some of the loss through its indirect exports of materials and intermediate goods to third countries that ship the end products to the US.

Considering both direct and indirect impacts of tariffs, we estimate a 1pp rise in tariff cut China's market share of US imports by 0.3pp in 2017-19 and 0.6pp in 2017-2023 - i.e., the loss in market share increased by 0.15pp each year before settling at 0.6pp.

China's direct and indirect exports to the US accounted for 20% of China's total exports in 2023, and total exports contributed about 20% of China's GDP. As a rule of thumb, we would expect a 1pp rise in the US tariff to knock off 0.04pp from China's GDP in the first year and a total of 0.15pp over a four-year period.

Amplifying the impact on the Chinese economy, further hikes in tariffs would be likely to curtail investment by discouraging FDI inflows and encouraging more ODI outflows to other countries to circumvent trade and non-trade barriers.

## Scenarios
We consider three main scenarios for Chinese imports to the US in the event that Trump wins the presidency:

* **Flat 60% tariff:** This would make it nearly prohibitive for China to export to the US. In the first year, we would expect China's market share of US imports to drop by 7.2pp, reducing China's GDP by 1.8%. From the second year onwards, China's exports to the US might shrink to negligible levels.

* **Removal of MFN:** We estimate the average tariff would spike from 12% currently to 45%, translating into declines of 6.2pp in China's exports and 1.2% in GDP in the first year. Starting from the second year, exports to the US would largely end.

* **Flat 25% tariff:** If the US extends the existing highest 25% punitive tariff on certain imports to all Chinese products, we estimate it would subtract 0.5% from GDP in the first year and a total of 1.5% in four years.

## Stimulus response
Depending on the magnitude of tariffs, China might respond in a number of ways:

* depreciating the currency to cushion the hit to exports;
* cutting interest rates and RRR to bolster the domestic economy;
* adopting bigger fiscal stimulus by issuing additional government bonds;
* exporting more to countries other than the US at lower prices, creating stronger disinflationary impulse for destination nations;
* imposing punitive tariffs on US imports;
* stepping up capacity reallocation to other countries in EM to avoid tariffs; and
* scaling back or halting official communications with the US.
