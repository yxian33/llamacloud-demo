

## Normalisation on the cards

As the energy shock continues to dissipate, we expect France's outperformance to end. We forecast GDP growth at 0.7% in 2024 and 1.4% in 2025, compared with 0.7% and 1.6% for the eurozone average.

While this is partially the result of some catch-up expected in other economies, such as Germany or Italy, we think French performance will be challenged by some idiosyncratic factors.

* **Greater exposure to the monetary shock:** We think the French economy will be more sensitive than its peers to the lagged impact of monetary tightening. This is because the country has relied on debt expansion to support growth over the past two decades. Private debt-to-GDP has increased by 53pp, reaching 142% in 2023 – the highest level among large eurozone economies (Figure 8).

* **Housing market adjustment:** Higher interest rates are set to slow the economy primarily through their impact on the housing market. Although they are not the most overvalued in Europe, French property prices were historically elevated before the c.3pp increase in mortgage rates observed since mid-2022.

Historically, when the housing market has suffered, so has the French economy. For instance, in 1992-95 and 2013-15, the economy recorded below-trend growth as the result of housing downturns.

* **More fiscal consolidation:** The French finance minister recently announced a cut to the government's GDP forecast for 2024, from 1.4% to 1.0%. To reach the deficit target, unchanged at 4.4% of GDP, he announced fresh public savings of EUR10bn. We estimate this will trim growth by an additional 0.1-0.2pp this year.

However, we still see reasons for optimism, with clear catch-up potential in consumer spending (page 7) and supported business investment (page 8).
