

## Labour market healing
The other success of policies carried out since 2014 has been the rise in the employment rate, up 4pp to a record-high in 2023. In addition, the share of hires on permanent contracts reached 52% last year, another record high. A stronger mobilisation of the population in the workforce should support potential growth.

## Productivity question
The flip side of the rise in employment rates has been a significant fall in productivity per worker. Productivity is currently around 5% below the pre-Covid level and 8% below the pre-Covid trend. To what extent this fall is permanent remains uncertain. We assume that around 4.5pp of the 8% productivity loss is permanent and around 3.5pp temporary. This would imply there are currently around 500,000 excess jobs if productivity catches up to its new trend. This could translate into a jobless recovery in the coming quarters.

## Skills deficit
French adults lag in international rankings of numeracy and digital skills. Raising productivity is therefore likely to require not only higher investment but also a long-term effort to upgrade skills and competences. The impact of education reforms can take decades.
