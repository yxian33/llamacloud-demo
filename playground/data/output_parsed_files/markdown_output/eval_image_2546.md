• Our forecasts for continued growth in US GDP are conditioned on the labour force participation rate remaining above its potential (computed by the CBO on the basis of demographic trends) and declining gradually.

• However, in recent years, the actual LFPR has dipped below that potential in several instances – for instance, in the decade following the Global Financial Crisis.

• Therefore, one risk is a faster drop than we assume in the LFPR (orange line in the bottom left-hand chart). The net impact would be lower GDP (both actual and potential) and higher inflation.

• The 'shape' of the drop in participation matters for the inflation impact. A more abrupt decline would be likely to cause a stronger rise in inflation than a more gradual decline.
