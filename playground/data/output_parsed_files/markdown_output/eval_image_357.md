* We expect Jerome Powell to chair the Fed Board of Governors until his term ends in 2026. His reappointment depends on the election outcome. In our view, though by then Powell might feel he has fulfilled his public duty.

* If Trump wins, he might continue public criticism of Powell, but he would have to have "cause" to remove any member of the Fed's board, which courts have interpreted as requiring a finding of inefficiency, neglect of duty or malfeasance.

* It is legally uncertain whether the president can demote the board's chair to be just one of the other governors. Were Trump to attempt that, the issue might have to go all the way to the Supreme Court – a lengthy and uncertain process.

* Existing laws grant the president relatively expansive powers to impose tariffs without congressional approval. However, such tariffs must still meet specified – albeit somewhat subjective – criteria.

* Given both these thresholds and a likely element of bureaucratic inertia, we would not expect a Trump presidency to result in an across-the-board tariff, or even a 60% tariff across all Chinese imports.

* Even a compromise figure such as a 20–25% tariff on all Chinese imports would constitute a significant increase from the roughly 12% average tariff rate paid on imports from China (for more, see next page).
