

## Depressed spending in goods
The inflation shock has led French households to cut back on spending on goods. For instance, spending on food is currently around 10% below the pre-Covid level, suggesting an attempt by households to keep spending constant in nominal terms. Spending on household equipment is also 17% below its pre-Covid trend. In our view, this creates catch-up potential in the medium run.

## Wealth accumulation
With disposable income protected by government support, French households have accumulated more financial wealth than European peers. For instance, in Q3 2023, total financial assets were 3.6% of GDP higher than in 2019, while currency and deposits were 8.3% of GDP higher.

## Rising real income growth and confidence
Real incomes have been growing since early 2023. We expect this to continue in 2024-25 as wage growth is likely to remain 1-1.5% higher than inflation. This should help consumer confidence to recover further.

## Only gradual decline in savings rate
Our forecasts assume a decline in the savings rate, currently at elevated levels compared with pre-Covid (18% in our estimate in Q4 2023 versus 15% in 2019).

However, this decline could be only gradual, as several drivers of the savings rate will go in the wrong direction in 2024.

* We expect the unemployment rate in the metropolitan area to pick up to around 8% by mid-2025 (from 7.3% in Q4 2023). While this would still be an historically low rate, the rise could lead to some precautionary savings.

* The lagged increase in electricity prices (close to 10% m/m in February) could delay the sentiment that the inflation crisis is over and contribute to higher savings in the short run.

* The ongoing decline in home prices could reduce non-financial wealth and lower propensity to spend. In France, two-thirds of households' wealth is made of housing assets.

The traditional drivers of the savings rate will be more positive in 2025, in our view, which could trigger an acceleration of consumer spending that year.
