

GLOBAL OUTLOOK Q2 2024

**China:** Policy easing also remains on the cards in China, where both structural and cyclical factors continue to weigh on growth. Our sense is that the authorities will continue their piecemeal and relatively cautious approach to easing policy. As a result, we forecast Chinese 2024 average GDP growth of just 4.5% – below the 5.0% GDP growth target set at the March National People's Congress – and see risks to our base case as skewed to the downside.

**Japan:** The outlier among major advanced economies is still Japan. With wage growth likely to stabilise closer to 4% y/y than 3%, we expect inflation to settle at levels well above the pre-Covid average, allowing the Bank of Japan to exit its negative interest rates policy and set a course for a gradual adjustment towards neutral.

While the language from the Bank of Japan will likely continue to suggest caution, we think conditions are in place for it to tighten more quickly than is currently priced in, with the IOER raised to at least 0.75% by end-2025.

**EM:** The picture in emerging markets remains relatively benign, supported by resilient growth in the US and a disinflationary trend. This should allow most central banks to continue to cut rates, albeit more cautiously from the second half of 2024 than has been the case so far. This is particularly true for economies in Central and Eastern Europe, where inflation could reaccelerate from Q2.

With still relatively restrictive real rates in ex-ante terms, Latam economies should continue to lead in the pace and magnitude of rate cuts this year, while in EM Asia the amplitude of monetary easing is likely to be as shallow on the way down as it was on the way up.

From the middle of 2024, the traditional central bank trade-off between growth, inflation and FX stability is likely to resurface as a common theme across EM, with currencies likely to weaken in response to monetary easing, most visibly in Asia and CEEMEA, as rate differentials tighten versus core markets.

**Key risks to our view:** A hawkish shift in central bank rhetoric on the back of more persistent inflation is the key risk to our global base case.
