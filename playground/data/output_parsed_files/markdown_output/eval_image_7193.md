

GLOBAL ECONOMICS | GLOBAL OUTLOOK Q2 2024

# Assumptions and risks

The last few years have proved challenging for forecasters due to the frequency and magnitude of unforeseen shocks that test the working assumptions underpinning our forecasts.

In this section we explore risks to our main global assumptions. Although we pay particular attention to two risks that have recently become topical — a drop in US labour force participation and more deflation expected by China — we think the US election is the most significant risk event that could challenge our central scenario.

## Geopolitics

Our outlook assumes that the conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine are not resolved but do not intensify either.

We see risks on both sides. An intensification of either conflict would likely cause a rise in energy prices. Oil prices could settle around 33% above our central scenario, which would fuel inflation, dampen growth and likely delay central banks' policy normalisation.

Conversely, a resolution of the conflicts would dampen uncertainty — in a differentiated way across regions — and could boost investment, while energy prices might settle lower than in our base case.

## Supply side

Our forecasts assume continued dissipation of the energy shock, with prices broadly in check, and higher labour force participation in the US, with a recent upward revision of its immigration estimates and projections.

If any of our assumptions fail, that would amount to a negative supply shock implying the ugly combination of higher inflation and lower growth, likely making central bankers more cautious in cutting rates.

## US election

We assume no material shift in policy following November's presidential and congressional elections.

In the event of a 'red wave' (the Republicans winning the presidency and both houses of Congress), fiscal policy might become more accommodative than we assume, leading to higher US growth and inflation and fewer Fed rate cuts.

The reimposition of trade tariffs is another risk, as it could contribute to higher prices while dampening growth.

## Chinese export prices

We forecast China's PPI to keep falling in 2024, albeit less than in 2023.

We see risks on both sides. Were Chinese producer prices to rise in 2024, this would represent a drag on global trade and pose a risk of higher inflation.

Conversely, even deeper PPI deflation in China would be positive for growth while curbing inflationary pressures.
