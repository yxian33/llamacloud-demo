* **Potential external shocks:** Geopolitical risks raise the prospect of renewed adverse supply shocks – for example, increases in transport prices or trade tariffs. Although rises in transport prices due to Red Sea tensions seem to have peaked, in our view, they imply upside risk to goods prices in Europe next year.

* **Stronger global demand:** Uncertainty about central banks' neutral rates (r-star) could result in looser-than-expected financial conditions, fuelling demand and preventing easing in labour markets (see page 21). Our modelling suggests that stronger US activity (net of tighter financial conditions) would be inflationary for EM even if China continues to export deflation.

* **Sticky wage growth:** If workers try to recover drops in real wages since early 2021, felt in all countries we cover, wage growth could stay sticky.

* **Corporates protecting margins:** The risk of price rises to bolster corporate profits is limited in Europe, elevated inventories/orders levels in PMI surveys suggest, but it looks higher in parts of EM (Latam and EM Asia) and the US.
