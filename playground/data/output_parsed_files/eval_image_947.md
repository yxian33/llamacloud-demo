* Lower export prices from China could have a positive effect on growth in other economies while curbing their inflation. For both growth and inflation, the impact would likely be larger on the US than the eurozone.

* The growth boost would likely depend on the nature of the shock affecting Chinese export prices.

* Our exercise assumes an exogenous decrease in prices. However, were lower prices to be driven by depressed growth in China instead, the growth impact on the rest of the world would probably be less positive.

* The Chinese PPI (and therefore export prices) tends to move in tandem with oil prices.

* Given that energy prices fell significantly in 2023, they are likely to contribute less to PPI deflation, which we think should moderate in 2024.

* However, another leg down in oil prices or stronger-than-expected efficiency gains in China might cause more PPI deflation than we forecast, leading to lower inflation in the rest of the world.
